How to play darts

Everything you need for your first game: how the board works, how to score and how to finish. Checkout Lab keeps the score — you just throw.

Start your first gameFull rules

In short

Everyone starts on 501 and throws three darts per turn. The total is subtracted. The first player to reach exactly zero — with the last dart on a double — wins.

The dartboard

Numbers 1–20

The board has 20 segments. The number on the outer ring is the segment value; 20 at the top is the most valuable.

Doubles and trebles

The thin outer ring doubles the score (D20 = 40), the thin inner ring trebles it (T20 = 60).

The bull

The bullseye is 50 and counts as a double; the ring around it is 25. The maximum visit is 180: three darts in T20.

How scoring works

After each three-dart visit the total is taken off: 501 − 60 = 441. Score more than you have left, leave 1, or reach zero without a double and it is a bust — the visit does not count. Enter the total in the scorer and it shows what is left and how to finish.

Stance and throw

Stance

Right-handers put the right foot forward at the oche, body slightly side-on. Weight on the front foot, shoulders still.

Grip

Hold the dart with three fingers at its centre of gravity, without squeezing. The tip points slightly up.

Throw

Only the forearm moves: back, then extend towards the target and follow through with the wrist.

How to start practising

For the first weeks aim at 20 and watch where your misses go. Then try Around the Clock — every number from 1 to 20 in order. Learn a few common finishes: 32 (D16), 40 (D20), 100 (T20 + D20). The full checkout table is always one tap away.

FAQ

What score do you start on in darts?

501 in the standard game, 301 in the shorter version. Each three-dart visit is subtracted.

What is a double in darts?

The thin outer ring, which doubles the segment score. With Double Out you must finish on a double or the bullseye.

How many darts per turn?

Three. The total of the three darts is called a visit.

Can you play darts alone?

Yes — practise on your own or play against the computer in Checkout Lab.
