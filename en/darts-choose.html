How to choose darts

Good darts will not play for you, but the wrong ones slow your progress. Weight, material and shape — without the marketing.

Test your darts in practiceDarts for beginners

In short

Start with 21–24 g tungsten darts with a medium straight barrel. Then experiment with shafts and flights — far cheaper than a new set.

Weight

Most players use 18 to 26 g; the rules allow up to 50 g. Lighter darts fly in a higher arc and reward a crisp throw. Heavier darts forgive an uneven action and stay stable, but tire the arm sooner. 22–23 g is a sensible middle to start from.

Barrel material

Brass

Cheap and tough, but thick: darts block each other in one segment. Fine for a first try.

Nickel-silver

Middle ground in price and thickness, common in budget sets.

Tungsten 80–97%

Dense metal: the same weight in a slimmer barrel, so darts group tighter in T20. Most players choose it.

Shape and grip

A straight barrel gives freedom of grip; a torpedo shifts weight forward for players who hold near the point. Knurling ranges from soft to aggressive: sweaty fingers need more grip, sore fingers less.

Shafts and flights

Short shafts with small flights fly fast and flat; longer shafts with standard flights stabilise the dart and forgive mistakes. They are consumables — replace flights when they bend.

How to test

If you can, try two or three sets and play a doubles round with each in Practice: the hit rate tells you more than the feel.

FAQ

What weight darts should a beginner use?

Usually 21–24 g — a middle point to move lighter or heavier from.

Why are tungsten darts better than brass?

Tungsten is denser, so the barrel is slimmer at the same weight and darts group tighter.

What is the maximum dart weight?

50 g under WDF and PDC rules, with a maximum length of 30.5 cm.

Do flights need replacing?

Yes — bent or torn flights change how the dart flies.
