501 darts rules

The rules of the classic game played in PDC events and every club. The Checkout Lab scorer applies them for you.

Play 501Double Out explained

The goal

Take your score from 501 to exactly zero before your opponent, finishing on a double or the bullseye.

Core rules

Who throws first

Players throw for the bull; the closest starts. After that, players alternate who starts each leg.

A visit

Three darts per turn, total subtracted from the score. Bounce-outs and fallen darts score nothing and are not re-thrown.

Bust

Going below zero, leaving 1, or reaching zero without a double cancels the visit and restores the score.

Checkout

The leg ends with a dart that brings the score to exactly zero on a double or the bull (50).

Legs and sets

One game from 501 to zero is a leg. Matches are first to a number of legs, e.g. best of 5. Big tournaments group legs into sets — a set is first to 3 legs, a match first to 3–7 sets. Checkout Lab supports both.

Disputed darts

On the wire

The segment where the point is embedded counts.

Foot over the oche

A dart thrown while over the line does not count.

Robin Hood

A dart stuck in another dart scores nothing.

Variants

301 is the shorter version, often with Double In: scoring starts after hitting a double. See the 301 scorer and other games.

FAQ

How does a game of 501 start?

Everyone starts on 501; throw for the bull to decide who starts the first leg.

What happens with 1 left?

It is a bust: 1 cannot be finished, so the visit does not count.

How many legs in a match?

Whatever you agree — best of 3, 5 or 7 legs is common. Tournaments use sets of 3 legs.

Does a bounce-out count?

No — it scores nothing and is not re-thrown.
